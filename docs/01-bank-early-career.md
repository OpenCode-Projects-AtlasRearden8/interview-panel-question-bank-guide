# Chapter 1 — Early-Career Question Bank

For hiring (or being) an **Intern, Junior Hire, New Graduate, First Job** —
including the flat roles that come first in most careers with an
`apply • no experience needed` line on the posting.

Each bank entry is written for one **side**. Facts and open questions
(`**Q:**`) belong to the hiring side — pick from the question list under the
appropriate **Stage** heading. Candidate-side entries include a
`**Calm-answer scaffold:**` line so you can practice the *exact* stage
versions of these questions in Chapter 4 worksheets.

## Hiring side — pick your stage and open

> The order is an arc: **Open with safe history → Learn what they do →
> Close soft**. Calibrated interviews ask every candidate in the same stage
> the same core questions.

### Stage 1 — Opening and background

- **Q: Q1. Tell me about yourself and what you'd like to do next.**
  - **Why it's included:** One question, so much signal. Opens the door to the
    candidate's own story instead of a yes/no, and reveals if they did homework
    (did they read the *about* section or just the skill list?).
  - **Listen for:** Specific detail ("I tutored algebra at the library on
    Saturdays"), not vibes ("I'm a people person"). Red flag: a *narrated*
    rehearsed-nothing — no detail and no link to the current role.
- **Q: Q2. What did your last job, class, or volunteer role prepare you for?**
  - **Why it's included:** New-career people often have *skills* but no
    *script*. This question forces them to translate a classroom or home task
    into the language of the job.
  - **Listen for:** Concrete examples. A rambler is workable; a stonewaller
    ("I guess nothing yet") in an opening question is a problem.
- **Q: Q3. Why this company, and not just any company that pays on Friday?**
  - **Why it's included:** First-jobbers usually don't have leverage and that's
    fine — calm answers are honest, not dramatic ("I don't know your company
    well, but I want a first job where I learn a routine I can carry").
  - **Listen for:** The detail. A hospital hire who can name one thing your
    clinic does differently beats a polished blanket answer.

### Stage 2 — Reliability, time, and growth

- **Q: Q4. What does showing up on time look like in a week for you?**
  - **Why it's included:** For first-jobbers, attendance is the skill. Asking
    *how they manage* a week (roster, alarms, rides, childcare) is fairer than
    a trick question about whether they'd ever be late.
  - **Listen for:** A plan, not a promise. Anyone can say "I'm very reliable."
    Strong: "I keep Sundays free and I never schedule the 7 a.m. shift with a
    ride I'd have to beg for."
- **Q: Q5. Tell me about a time you had to learn something fast with no
  supervision. What did you do first?**
  - **Why it's included:** Predicts trainability. Early-career hires are hired
    for roughly zero knowledge and a good slope.
  - **Listen for:** An actual *method* ("I Googled it, then asked the senior
    tech after I tried once"), not false confidence.
- **Q: Q6. What's something you're actively bad at — but is cheap to fix?**
  - **Why it's included:** Self-honesty calibrated to impact. New hires who
    can name a fixable gap and describe the fix are usually the ones we keep.
  - **Listen for:** Small, cheap, real. "I can't read a paper to save my life"
    is a bad pick (job-critical); "I've never used a spreadsheet"
    is a fine one.

### Stage 3 — Teamwork and feedback

- **Q: Q7. Tell me about a coworker, classmate, or customer who frustrated
  you. What did you do?**
  - **Why it's included:** Conflict *will* happen. This question shows what
    the candidate does with friction: escalate, absorb, fix, or gossip.
  - **Listen for:** A step they took. Red flag: "I just stay quiet" —
    quiet can be fine, but with no repair step it usually isn't.
- **Q: Q8. How do you take a hard piece of feedback?**
  - **Why it's included:** First-jobbers get more feedback than they've ever
    had. If they flinch at the *word* "feedback," the job will hurt.
  - **Listen for:** "I ask for an example and try it once" beats
    "I take it very personally" (weak but honest) — and beats
    "I've never needed feedback" (not honest).

### Stage 4 — Close and wrap

- **Q: Q9. If you had this job tomorrow, what would you do in the first hour?**
  - **Why it's included:** The classic "no wrong answer but a revealing one"
    close. It tests whether they can imagine the *work*, not just the badge.
  - **Listen for:** "Ask my lead what's behind on today" or "find the mop"
    are fine calm answers; "overtime pay rates" is a fine question to *ask*,
    but not a first-hour plan.
- **Q: Q10. What would make you quit a first month in?**
  - **Why it's included:** Saves both of you. Sleep-safe teams need this on
    the record. It also tests handle on reality: every job has dull hours.
  - **Listen for:** Safety and honesty ("if I'm asked to falsify training logs
    I'm gone") are strong; "if you don't let me pick my schedule" is
    negotiable-talk, not a red flag, if stated calmly.

## Candidate side — the four calm answers

> You will likely be asked three of these ten questions. Pick the **calm
> answer worksheet** version of each (Chapter 4) and write it out like a
> normal paragraph. Stop when it's two to four sentences. Then stop.

### Candidate entry A — Opening

- **Q: Q11. Tell me about yourself and what you'd like to do next.**
  - **Calm-answer scaffold:**

      - I'm a ________ (student / just-finished / switching from ________).
      - The part of my last ________ I did well: ________.
      - I don't have the title yet, but here's what I can do on day one:
        ________ — and that's why this role looked right.

- **Q: Q12. What did your last job, class, or volunteer role prepare you for?**
  - **Calm-answer scaffold:**

      - The boring-sounding class/job I want credit for is ________.
      - Three things it made me good at: ________, ________, ________.
      - The one of those this job needs most: ________.

### Candidate entry B — Reliability and learning

- **Q: Q13. What does showing up on time look like in a week for you?**
  - **Calm-answer scaffold:**

      - My week: days I'm fully open: ________. Days that need a workaround:
        ________, and my workaround is ________.
      - One specific thing I've done to make mornings boring (alarm, bus,
        packed bag): ________.

- **Q: Q14. Tell me about a time you had to learn something fast.**
  - **Calm-answer scaffold:**

      - I had to learn ________ with no warning.
      - First I tried ________; it half-worked.
      - What actually fixed it: ________.
      - What I'd do differently next time: ________.

### Candidate entry C — Team, feedback, and close

- **Q: Q15. Tell me about a coworker or customer who frustrated you.**
  - **Calm-answer scaffold:**

      - The conflict was about ________ (keep it one sentence, not a saga).
      - What I actually did: ________.
      - How it ended: ________ quietly/awkwardly/fine — be honest.

- **Q: Q16. What would make you quit in the first month?**
  - **Calm-answer scaffold:**

      - Hard line (safety/legal/honesty): ________.
      - Negotiable friction I'd raise in week one instead of quitting:
        ________.

> The calm answer to every one of these is the same shape: **one true
> sentence, one specific detail, and stop.** If your draft paragraph has a
> story arc and a punchline, you've written too much. Chapter 4 does this
> on paper, in plain lines.
