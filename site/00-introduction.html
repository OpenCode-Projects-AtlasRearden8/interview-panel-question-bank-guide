How to Use This Workbook

Welcome. This is a workbook, not a book you read straight through and shelve. You are meant to have a pen (or a text editor) and a marker for bleed-through.

Who this is for

Both sides of the table. If you are interviewing candidates for a role, the question banks in Chapters 1–3 give you a starting list that is fair, job-relevant, and easy to score consistently. If you are the candidate, the same chapters show you what a well-run panel is likely to ask and what a "listen-for" actually sounds like from the other side.

Chapters 1–3 each contain a question bank: numbered questions split into sections and stages, each with a note on why it's included and what a good listener hears. One bank, two sides. The "Ask when" line under the hiring side tells the interviewer when to open with the question; the candidate side gives prompts and scaffolds to draft a calm answer to the most likely versions of the same question.

The calm answer idea

Most interview advice pushes candidates to perform louder than they feel. This workbook pushes the opposite direction: your calm answer is short, specific, and stops talking before it rambles. Chapter 4 has worksheets with scaffold lines for you to fill in — there are no pre-written "right answers" here, because the calm answer that works is one you actually wrote in your own plain words.

Page structure conventions

To help the build script (and your eyes) find things quickly:

  • Bank chapters (1–3) use numbered question bullets under headings that say which bank and side they belong to. Each entry looks like:
    - **Q: Q1. Tell me about yourself and what you'd like to do next.**
      - **Why it's included:** ...
      - **Listen for:** ...
  • **Q:** is the question itself, numbered by hand (Q1., Q2., …).
  • **Why it's included:** gives the interviewer's reasoning — why this question earns its slot in the arc.
  • **Listen for:** tells you what an honest strong answer sounds like.
  • **Calm-answer scaffold:** (candidate side) is a fill-in outline, not a script — you write the words in Chapter 4 style.
  • Worksheets use fill-in lines: Ask when: ________ means "the point in the interview arc where this question fits best."

Privacy note: every fill-in page in this workbook is yours. Write local files, use local pens, and never paste interview notes into a cloud tool you did not choose on purpose. Chapter 6 explains how to run a mock interview with no API key and no data leaving your machine.

  • Reference points: Glossary has plain definitions for "behavioral question", "calibrated rubric", "listen-for", and other terms used through the workbook.

Suggested next step: flip to the chapter that matches your next interview (Chapters 1–3), pick three questions, and draft them in a worksheet printout.
