# How to Use This Workbook Welcome. This is a workbook, not a book you read straight through and shelve. You are meant to have a pen (or a text editor) and a marker for bleed-through. ## Who this is for **Both sides of the table.** If you are interviewing candidates for a role, the question banks in Chapters 1–3 give you a starting list that is fair, job-relevant, and easy to score consistently. If you are *the candidate*, the same chapters show you what a well-run panel is likely to ask and what a "listen-for" actually sounds like from the other side. ## What is inside | Chapter | Title | Use it when | |---|---|---| | 1 | Early-Career Question Bank | Hiring or applying for Intern, Junior, New Grad, First Job | | 2 | Healthcare Question Bank | Hiring or applying for CNA, MA, LPN/RN, Front Office, Tech | | 3 | Small-Business Question Bank | Hiring or applying for Retail, Café, Office Assistant, Bookkeeper | | 4 | Calm-Answer Worksheets | You have questions and need to draft answers (or ask them calmly) | | 5 | Fairness Notes | Before you finalize your question list or score sheet | | 6 | AI Mock Sessions | You want practice interviews with a local AI model | | 7 | Score-Sheet Templates | You need a one-page scoring sheet for a panel | | 8 | Glossary | A term made you pause | Chapters 1–3 each contain a **question bank**: numbered questions split into *sections* and *stages*, each with a note on **why it's included** and **what a good listener hears**. One bank, two sides. The "Ask when" line under the hiring side tells the interviewer when to open with the question; the candidate side gives prompts and scaffolds to draft a calm answer to the most likely versions of the same question. ## The calm answer idea Most interview advice pushes candidates to perform louder than they feel. This workbook pushes the opposite direction: your **calm answer** is short, specific, and stops talking before it rambles. Chapter 4 has worksheets with scaffold lines for you to fill in — there are no pre-written "right answers" here, because the calm answer that works is one you actually wrote in your own plain words. ## Page structure conventions To help the build script (and your eyes) find things quickly: - **Bank chapters** (1–3) use numbered question bullets under headings that say which *bank* and *side* they belong to. Each entry looks like: ``` - **Q: Q1. Tell me about yourself and what you'd like to do next.** - **Why it's included:** ... - **Listen for:** ... ``` - `**Q:**` is the question itself, numbered by hand (`Q1.`, `Q2.`, …). - `**Why it's included:**` gives the interviewer's reasoning — why this question earns its slot in the arc. - `**Listen for:**` tells you what an honest strong answer sounds like. - `**Calm-answer scaffold:**` (candidate side) is a fill-in outline, not a script — you write the words in Chapter 4 style. - Worksheets use fill-in lines: `Ask when: ________` means "the point in the interview arc where this question fits best." > **Privacy note:** every fill-in page in this workbook is yours. Write > local files, use local pens, and never paste interview notes into a cloud > tool you did not choose on purpose. Chapter 6 explains how to run a mock > interview with **no API key and no data leaving your machine**. - Reference points: [Glossary](08-glossary.md) has plain definitions for "behavioral question", "calibrated rubric", "listen-for", and other terms used through the workbook. *Suggested next step:* flip to the chapter that matches your next interview (Chapters 1–3), pick **three questions**, and draft them in a worksheet printout.
