Chapter 7 — Score-Sheet Templates

Two printable templates follow. Make one copy per candidate, and one per panelist — every panelist scores independently before any discussion (Chapter 5 explains why). Fill the anchored-scale row in before the first interview of the day, not after.

Template 2 — Single-interviewer small-business sheet

For the 20-minute counter interview at a shop: five questions, quick anchors, legal-friendly, and short enough to fill in between candidates.

Question (same five every time)1–5Note (behavior only)
Customer-wrong story (Q1, Ch. 3)
"I'll find out" habit (Q2, Ch. 3)
Which-hat dread honesty (Q3, Ch. 3)
Money-mistake honesty (Q4, Ch. 3)
Day-1 ask-then-act plan (Q6, Ch. 3)

Notes that stay off the sheet (privacy and fairness): family status, health, attractiveness ("tech-bro haircut" counts), anything about where a person is "originally" from. Write only what a stranger could read aloud in a room with the candidate's lawyer standing comfortably in the doorway.

Calibrating between panelists

  • If two panelists differ by more than one point on the same answer, discuss the behavior, not the person: "What did you hear that I didn't?"
  • Keep one sheet per question, not one per candidate summary. The mismatch is almost always within a single answer, and the fix is an anchor rewrite, not a panelist re-education.
  • Once per hiring round, cut the weakest question and rewrite its anchored scale. Sheets that never change stop measuring anything after a while.
